Kelly Bankroll Lab

Two sides of the same math: how a bettor should size bets when they have an edge, and how a sportsbook defends against the bettors who size them right. Everything runs live in your browser.

  • Calibration is money. A 2 point overestimate at −110 turns full Kelly into a 4x overbet.
  • Limits turn compounding into a line. A capped sharp costs the book a fixed amount per bet, not a growing one.
  • The close is the scoreboard. CLV proves an edge in far fewer bets than win and loss records can.

04

Calibration: when your model is overconfident

The market posts −110 on both sides, treating every game as 50/50. Each game has a true win chance that differs from 50% by a few points. Your model sees that difference, but scaled by a calibration slope: above 1 means overconfident, so your model pushes probabilities too far from 50%. You bet whichever side your model likes, sized by Kelly on your model.

You are the risk desk

07

Parlays vs straight bets

A parlay multiplies whatever edge each leg has. For a bettor with an edge that sounds great until you size it. For a book facing a bettor with no edge, it multiplies the hold, which is why parlays are such a big profit line.

08

CLV pricing tool

The closing line is the sharpest price the market makes. Remove the vig to get a fair probability, compare it with the price you got, and you have an edge estimate. How you remove the vig matters, and so does how many bets it takes to prove anything.

09

Methods and caveats

Kelly fraction. With win chance p, net odds b (profit per $1) and q = 1 − p, Kelly bets f* = (pb − q) / b of the current bankroll. It maximizes expected log growth per bet, g(f) = p ln(1 + bf) + q ln(1 − f). After T bets the median bankroll is about eTg.

Main simulation. Each path draws one win and loss sequence from a seeded generator, and every strategy bets that same sequence, so differences come from sizing and not luck. Paths run in a Web Worker. Busted means below 1% of the starting bankroll. The race shows the path where full Kelly finishes at the chosen percentile.

Strategies. Full, fractional and 2x Kelly bet a share of current bankroll. Capped Kelly bets full Kelly up to a dollar max. Flat bets a fixed dollar amount. Martingale, in the race only, doubles after each loss and resets after a win.

Exact sections. The heatmap, growth curve and histogram bins rely on the fact that with a fixed fraction, final wealth depends only on the number of wins, which is binomial. The slate uses a one factor Gaussian copula: each game wins when a correlated normal falls below its threshold, integrated on a grid.

Calibration lab. True game logits are normal around 0 (50%). Your model logit is slope times the true logit plus optional noise. Brier score and log loss are averaged over 20,000 games. With noise, picking the games your model likes most creates the winner's curse: noise becomes bias.

Risk desk. Rec handle and P&L per game use a normal approximation to the sum of lognormal stakes, with recs losing at the posted price on fair 50/50 games. Sharps bet in rotating order and each sees the line moved by earlier sharp money. Observed CLV is the sharp's true edge at their price plus noise. Both scenarios use identical random draws.

Caveats. Real bets are not independent coin flips, edges drift, and real books also move lines on rec flow. Parlay EV here assumes the book pays the posted prices multiplied together and legs are independent; same game parlays are correlated and books price them with that correlation, usually worse for the bettor. The realistic NFL preset (about 1.5% edge) is an assumption, not a measured result.

Devig methods. Multiplicative scales both implied probabilities by their sum. Additive subtracts half the overround from each. Power raises both to the exponent that makes them sum to 1. Shin assumes a share of insider money and shifts more of the margin onto the longshot.
