Test 1 · Is the line unbiased?No. 03

Spread in, margin out

If the spread is a fair price, a team favored by 6 should win by about 6 on average. Regress the actual home margin on the closing spread: a perfect market gives a slope of 1 and an intercept of 0.

Test 2 · Key numbersNo. 04

Football scores are lumpy

Points come in 3s and 7s, so final margins pile up on a few numbers. That is why half a point around 3 or 7 is worth real money, and why books shade the juice there instead of moving the line.

Test 3 · Calibration · 2006 onNo. 05

When Vegas says 70%, is it 70%?

Strip the vig out of each moneyline to get a fair win probability, group teams by that number, and check how often they actually won. Dots on the red line mean the price was honest. Moneylines exist in this data from 2006 on.

Return per $1 bet on every team in each bucket at the posted moneyline, with bootstrap 95% intervals. Long shots are the worst deal on the board (the favorite longshot bias), though the intervals are wide.

Test 8 · Season by seasonNo. 10

The market keeps learning

Home field used to be worth about two and a half points in the line. After the empty stadium season of 2020 the market cut it to about one and a half and never went back.
