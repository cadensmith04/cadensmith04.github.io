The objective

Pick nine players (x is 1 if picked) to maximize

μᵀx − λ · xᵀΣx

λ above zero pays to cut variance (cash mindset). λ below zero pays for variance (tournament mindset, where only the top tail wins money). The quadratic term is linearized with one helper variable per correlated pair, so the whole thing is a mixed integer program.

Reading the grid

Each square is the correlation of surprise points (actual minus projection) between two roster roles in the same game, pooled over every game before the selected week and shrunk toward zero so thin samples do not shout. WR1 is a team's top receiver by trailing points.

Stacking a QB with his receivers piles positively correlated assets together. Variance goes up without the mean going down, which a tournament wants and a cash game avoids.

The operator's view

Running the contest, not entering it

Guarantee and overlay risk

Prize pool = entries × fee × (1 − rake). If a site guarantees a prize pool and too few people enter, the site pays the gap (the overlay). Entries are uncertain, modeled as lognormal around the expected count.

expected overlay ($) chance of overlay

Duplicate lineups split the prize

Identical lineups that tie split the prize money for the places they occupy. Popular builds are worth less than their score suggests, which is why ownership matters in tournaments and why a site watches duplication when it designs contests.

Under the hood

Methods and caveats

Show methods and caveats
  • Data: nflverse weekly player and team stats plus schedules with closing Vegas lines, 2024 to 2026. Slates are Sunday main slates (1pm and 4pm ET kickoffs). FanDuel scoring (half PPR, −2 per fumble lost, no yardage bonuses) or DraftKings scoring (full PPR, 300/100/100 bonuses).
  • Projection: exponentially weighted average of past games (halflife 4), shrunk toward a trailing position baseline, then scaled by (Vegas implied total ÷ the team's recent scoring) to the power 0.5. That elasticity fit 2024 best among 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1. Only information from before kickoff.
  • Risk: player SD is his residual SD shrunk toward his position's. Correlations are pooled by roster role pair within a game and shrunk toward zero. Σ = D R D. Simulations use a Gaussian copula on R with empirical residuals, which keeps the long right tail.
  • Optimizer: mixed integer program. FanDuel: $60,000, QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, TE, FLEX, DEF, at most 4 players per team. DraftKings: $50,000, same slots, no defense facing your own players. HiGHS in Python and in WebAssembly here.
  • Salaries are synthetic. Real historical salaries are not openly available. Each price is a position floor plus a dollar rate per point of trailing 8 game average, capped near real ranges. The market is slower than the projection, which is the edge the optimizer chases, so dollar results flatter the model.
  • The field is a model. 1,000 entries from 110 seed lineups: 50% sharp optimizers with mixed risk appetite, 30% forced stackers, 20% public players chasing recent averages with noisier projections, each entry tweaked by up to two random swaps. No real ownership data.
  • Hindsight on injuries. The slate only contains players who ended up playing. nflverse also omits games where a player recorded no stats, which trims the downside and makes SDs a bit too low.
  • Small sample. One lineup per strategy per week. Point differences between strategies are a few standard errors at most, and dollar results hinge on a handful of big finishes.
